Geospatial Analysis: Corridor Breach Findings

Methodology: A 3-mile proximity buffer (represented by the translucent green circles) was calculated around core elk and deer winter concentration zones to map heavy foraging boundaries. Findings: This overlay analysis reveals a clear geospatial pattern: highway segments slicing directly through an active winter concentration buffer zone (such as US-36 slicing through the Estes Valley Basin) register significantly higher animal-vehicle collision rates (29–42 annual incidents) compared to transport corridors that route entirely clear of herd ranges. This pinpoints critical coordinates where state planners should construct wildlife overpasses or underpasses to preserve migration safety.
